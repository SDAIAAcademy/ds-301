How defences are scored

The rubric

Specification
20%
Diagnostics
30%
Uncertainty
20%
Interpretation
20%
Reproducibility
10%
Every check with its number. A diagnostic named without its evidence and its fix scores as not done.
Full marks look like this. Specification: use and estimand stated, terms and family justified from the data. Diagnostics: at least three checks with numbers, what failed and what changed. Uncertainty: every effect with an interval, at the right level of clustering. Interpretation: reader's units, no causal word the design cannot carry. Reproducibility: the notebook reruns from the raw data with a fixed seed.
Format

8 minutes of defence, then 4 minutes of questions

1 · The brief

The question, the use and the estimand. One slide or one README section.

2 · The model and its checks

Terms, family, diagnostics: what failed and what you changed.

3 · Effects and limits

In the reader's units with intervals, and what they do not mean.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.
Participants may present from the README itself; slides are optional. The repository, not the slides, is what is scored.
The 4 minutes of questions

The assessor changes one term or the family; you predict the result before re-running

  • "Drop the interaction: what happens to the villa effect?"
  • "Use Poisson instead: which interval narrows?"
  • "Remove the district term: what happens to the programme's standard error?"

What is scored

The direction and rough size of the change, and the reason. A wrong prediction with the right reasoning still earns credit.

Pick the change from the participant's own model before the defence. Allow one re-run of the notebook. This is the live check that the work is the participant's own, since the capstone is open to an assistant.
